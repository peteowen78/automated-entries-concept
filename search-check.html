Can people find Platinum Automated Entries online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

2 of 10

Your site passes 2 of the 10 basic checks. Google is allowed to list you and your contact details are written out, but eight of the ten basics are missing. The one that matters most is that your site has no secure connection.

The one that matters most

Your website has no secure connection, and visitors are warned about it.

Your site only opens at an address beginning http, without the s that marks a secure connection. Web browsers label pages like this "Not secure", and newer browsers are starting to show a warning page before they open them at all.

If someone types the secure version of your address, it is worse: your web host answers with a security certificate belonging to a different company's website, so the browser warns that the site may not be what it claims. This is most likely a setting at your web host rather than anything you have done.

For a firm that installs security gates and intercoms, that first impression matters. A new site would run on a secure connection from day one, so the padlock shows and nobody is warned away.

Seven smaller things to fix

None of these is difficult. Together they decide whether you show up when someone nearby searches for electric gates.

To fix

Every page is called "Home"

The title of a page is the blue link people click in a search result. All fourteen of your pages are titled just "Home", including Wooden Gates, Steel Gates, Intercoms and Contact Us. Nothing says Platinum, gates or Cheshire.

A new site would give each page its own title: made to measure wooden gates, wrought iron gates, gate automation and intercoms, from Antrobus near Northwich.

To fix

Google is left to write your search listing

Under the blue link in a search result there are two lines describing the business. Your site does not supply them on any page, so Google picks words from wherever it can.

A new site would write them: bespoke gates and gate automation, designed, made and fitted across Cheshire, with a free site survey.

To fix

Shared links show nothing

When someone shares your site with a neighbour or in a village group, no picture, title or description goes with the link. You have dozens of fine photographs of your gates; none of them is used.

A new site would attach one of your best gate photographs and one clear line to every share.

To fix

Your main heading is your logo, labelled "logo"

Every page should have one main heading that says what the business is. It is the first thing a search engine reads. On your site that heading is the logo picture, and its only description is the word "logo".

A new site would open with words: bespoke wooden and iron gates, automated and fitted, Antrobus, Cheshire.

To fix

Over a hundred gate photographs, none described

Pictures can carry a short written description, used by image searches and read aloud to people who cannot see the screen. Across your gallery pages there are more than a hundred photographs of your work and not one has a description. Most of those pages have no words at all, just a heading and the pictures.

A new site would describe each one in plain words, such as "timber swing gates with automation on a gravel drive", so your own work can be found in image searches.

To fix

Google is not handed your details

A site can give Google a small fact sheet about the business: what it does, its address, its phone number and its opening hours. Yours does not have one.

A new site would include it, with your Antrobus workshop, both numbers and your Monday to Saturday hours.

To fix

No map of your pages for search engines

Search engines are helped by a simple list of every page on the site, kept at a standard address. Yours does not have one; the Sitemap link at the foot of your page is a list for people, not the kind search engines look for.

A new site would create one automatically and keep it up to date.

Can AI assistants find you?

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers instead of searching. These tools read your website too. We checked four things they rely on. Your site passes 3 of 4.

Working

AI assistants are allowed in

Nothing on your site turns them away.

Working

The words can be read without running the page

The words on your home page are there in the page itself, so AI tools can read them. Your gallery pages have almost no words, so there is little for them to read about your work.

Working

Your phone number and address are written out

Your phone numbers, address and postcode are written on your home page.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

There is none. It is a newer idea, a short plain-text summary of what you make and fit, the area you cover and how to get a free survey, and it takes about twenty minutes to write.

A few plain sentences on each gallery page, saying what the gate is made of and what it does, would give AI tools far more to go on.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.
